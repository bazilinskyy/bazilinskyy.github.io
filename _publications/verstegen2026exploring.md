abstract: "Automated vehicles (AVs) are becoming reality, and their interactions with other road users (ORUs) can evoke human uncertainties, which could be addressed using external human-machine interfaces. This paper explores the perception of uncertainty from the perspective of ORUs interacting with an AV. Two scenarios of interactions with an AV were generated by the participants, and four pre-defined scenarios were shown with virtual reality. Using the Repertory Grid Technique, 25 participants compared these traffic scenarios to identify key factors influencing perceived uncertainty. Inductive qualitative content analysis of the factors identified by participants demonstrates that the environment, signaling, subjective evaluations, behavior and performance, and social dynamics influence uncertainty. We further assess the level of influence and uniqueness of these factors for interactions with AVs. Based on the findings, we discuss three key implications: (1) the diversity of factors and how addressing those requires a holistic approach, (2) the potential role of adaptations in the built environment and (3) the need for standardization to address uncertainties."
